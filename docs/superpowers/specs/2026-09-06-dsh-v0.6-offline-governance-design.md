# DSH v0.6 Offline Governance Design

## Status

Proposed first v0.6 slice, revised after Sol/high and Astra reviews. This design starts controlled Self-Evolution as an offline governance capability; it does not activate runtime policy mutation.

## Goal

Turn the inert v0.5 candidate hypotheses into versioned, independently evaluated, human-approved promotion and rollback records while preserving the existing keyless and provider-disabled boundary.

## Scope

v0.6 first implements a pure governance layer in `@ds-plugins/dsh-eval`:

- a bounded policy-artifact reference and version schema;
- an authoritative evaluator policy for the first governed surface;
- explicit paired base/variant fixture evidence with domain-qualified run references;
- a full candidate snapshot whose identity and surface are independently verified;
- explicit `proposed`, `approved`, `rejected`, `promoted`, and `rolled-back` ledger states;
- immutable human decision records;
- deterministic hash-chained history with derived active and last-known-good pointers;
- offline tests over checked-in fixtures and replay evidence.

The first governed surface is `template`, mapped only from `CandidateV1.kind === 'prompt'`. The schema remains extensible to `routing`, `verification`, and `kernel`, but those surfaces are rejected by the first evaluator unless a separately versioned evidence policy is supplied.

## Non-goals

- No resident Self-Evolution plugin.
- No provider calls, credentials, network access, quota endpoint, or cost endpoint.
- No automatic runtime Profile, prompt, route, permission, sandbox, or verification-policy mutation.
- No online A/B assignment or exposure measurement; the first A/B comparison is paired offline replay over identical fixture inputs.
- No physical worker isolation, trusted write capture, or transactional workspace integration.
- No raw prompts, source text, model output, secrets, or private telemetry payloads in governance records.
- No reuse of the existing v0.2 `PromotionReportV1` as the complete v0.6 governance contract. It remains evaluation evidence for its original fixed corpus.

## Design choices

### 1. Pure governance subpath

The governance layer belongs beside the existing offline metrics and candidate generation, but it is exposed through a dedicated `@ds-plugins/dsh-eval/governance` subpath. The subpath imports only pure governance modules, canonical serialization, and the validated telemetry contract types it needs; it must not import the main `dsh-eval` entry, runtime evaluators, Cordis, code-intelligence runtime code, filesystem writers, provider adapters, or modules that also export filesystem-writing helpers.

In particular, governance must not import or call `telemetry/candidates.ts`, `telemetry/lessons.ts`, `buildCandidates`, `buildLessons`, `mineFailures`, `writeLesson`, or any fixture builder that reaches those modules. The governance package implements its own pure candidate-support resolver over supplied snapshots and telemetry records. Test fixture construction is pure and in-memory. The ledger consumes validated `CandidateV1` values and immutable evaluation evidence, returns new values, and has no Cordis lifecycle. File publication, signatures, and an artifact store are later concerns and must consume the same validated contracts rather than bypassing them.

```text
dsh-telemetry/contracts  ->  dsh-eval/governance
                                      |
                                      +-- pure contracts, evaluator policy, ledger
                                      +-- no runtime writer or provider import
```

### 2. Artifact references, not executable policy bodies

The first slice records opaque content-addressed artifact references. A reference contains a governed surface, semantic version, and SHA-256 digest of the canonical artifact supplied by an external authoring step. The ledger never interprets or executes the body.

### 3. Evaluator-owned policy

The first policy is the checked-in `template-offline-v1` evaluator policy. Its metric inventory, requiredness, availability rules, comparison formulas, directions, and thresholds are compiled constants identified by `(policyId, policyRevision, policyDigest)`. An input record carries only that policy identity; it cannot supply `required`, `threshold`, `passes`, or the overall result.

The evaluator derives all comparison statuses from the policy and arm observations. The parser rejects unknown policy identities and rejects any serialized derived field that does not equal recomputation under the known policy. An unavailable required metric produces `incomplete`, never `passed`.

The normative `template-offline-v1` metric policy is:

| Metric | Required | Permitted arm basis | Minimum coverage (`observedRuns / eligibleRuns`) | Arm value formula | Direction and comparison | Pass threshold | Zero-denominator behavior |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| `task_success_rate` | yes | `observed` | `1.0` | successful known-outcome annotations / known-outcome annotations | higher is better; `delta = variantValue - baseValue` | `delta >= 0` | arm is `unavailable`; because required, overall is at best `incomplete` |
| `accepted_result_rate` | yes | `observed` | `1.0` | annotations with `accepted === true` / annotations with explicit boolean `accepted` | higher is better; `delta = variantValue - baseValue` | `delta >= 0` | arm is `unavailable`; because required, overall is at best `incomplete` |
| `verification_cost` | no | `observed` | `0.8` | sum of `durationMs` on `verification-finished` observations / runs having at least one such observation | lower is better; `delta = variantValue - baseValue` | `variantValue <= baseValue * 1.10` | comparison is `unavailable`; optional unavailability does not by itself prevent `passed` |

For every row, `eligibleRuns` is the number of complete prescribed fixture runs in that arm. The numerator, denominator, `observedRuns`, `eligibleRuns`, basis, and value are recomputed from resolved observations and annotations; supplied values are assertions only. `value` must equal `numerator / denominator` without rounding when the denominator is positive. If the denominator is zero, the only canonical observation is `{ value: null, numerator: 0, denominator: 0, observedRuns: 0, eligibleRuns, basis: 'unavailable' }`. A nonzero denominator with a disallowed basis or coverage below the row minimum makes that arm unavailable for comparison. The evaluator requires exactly these three metric names in this order and rejects extras, duplicates, and omissions.

Overall result precedence is exact: structural or derived-field inconsistency rejects the evidence; otherwise any available metric failure yields `failed`; otherwise incomplete corpus coverage, an incomplete/lossy run, or an unavailable required metric yields `incomplete`; otherwise the result is `passed`. An unavailable optional metric is ignored only at the last step.

`policyDigest` is the lowercase SHA-256 of canonical JSON for the following digest input, with `corpusManifestDigest` first recomputed as specified below. Property ordering is supplied by canonical serialization, not by source layout:

```ts
{
  schemaVersion: 1,
  policyId: 'template-offline-v1',
  policyRevision: 1,
  corpus: {
    corpusId: 'template-offline-v1-corpus',
    corpusRevision: '1',
    corpusManifestDigest: sha256(canonicalJson(corpusManifestBody)),
  },
  metrics: [
    { name: 'task_success_rate', required: true, permittedBasis: ['observed'], minimumCoverage: 1,
      formula: 'successful-known-outcomes/known-outcomes', direction: 'higher', threshold: { deltaGte: 0 },
      zeroDenominator: 'required-unavailable' },
    { name: 'accepted_result_rate', required: true, permittedBasis: ['observed'], minimumCoverage: 1,
      formula: 'accepted-true/explicit-acceptance', direction: 'higher', threshold: { deltaGte: 0 },
      zeroDenominator: 'required-unavailable' },
    { name: 'verification_cost', required: false, permittedBasis: ['observed'], minimumCoverage: 0.8,
      formula: 'verification-duration-ms/runs-with-verification-duration', direction: 'lower',
      threshold: { variantLteBaseTimes: 1.1 }, zeroDenominator: 'optional-unavailable' },
  ],
  requiredPairCoverage: 1,
  overallResultPrecedence: ['reject-invalid', 'failed', 'incomplete', 'passed'],
}
```

### 4. Explicit paired arms

The checked-in `template-offline-v1-corpus` manifest is authoritative. Its body contains a non-empty, canonically sorted list of unique `(fixtureId, fixtureRevision, pairingKey)` tuples; its `corpusManifestDigest` is SHA-256 over the canonical manifest body excluding the digest. The policy accepts only corpus ID `template-offline-v1-corpus`, corpus revision `1`, and the digest recomputed from the supplied manifest. Changing, duplicating, reordering non-canonically, or substituting a fixture changes or invalidates the manifest and therefore the policy identity.

`resolveOfflineEvidence` is a pure function from `OfflineEvidenceResolverInputV1` to `EvaluationEvidenceV1`. Its input contains the authoritative manifest and complete in-memory base and variant run snapshots. It performs no file reads, writes, environment access, clock reads, network calls, or provider calls. Each arm must contain exactly one run for every manifest tuple and no unprescribed run. Each pair must contain exactly one base/variant binding for every manifest tuple. A fixture omitted symmetrically from both arms is still absent relative to the manifest and yields `incomplete`, not a smaller passing corpus.

A run is complete only when its domain-qualified reference matches every observation, its one seal, and its one annotation; the seal is present, has `complete === true`, `lostCount === 0`, and `observationCount === observations.length`; observation ordinals are unique and contiguous from 1 through `observationCount`; and every annotation evidence reference resolves to an observation in the same `(domainRef, runRef)`. Missing annotations, missing seals, lossy seals, count mismatches, ordinal gaps, cross-domain records, or unresolved annotation evidence make the arm incomplete. The resolver derives fixture, resolved, complete, and excluded counts (`excludedRuns` is always zero in this policy), arm metrics, pairs, comparisons, and the overall result. Serialized copies of those fields are rejected if altered.

The initial paired offline A/B record is not an online exposure claim. It means both artifacts were evaluated against the same prescribed inputs under the same keyless harness.

### 5. Candidate snapshot and surface mapping

The proposal embeds the complete canonical `CandidateV1` snapshot, not only its ID, plus a `CandidateSupportV1` snapshot containing the complete lesson/pattern and the resolved supporting runs. The pure candidate-support resolver recomputes pattern ID, lesson ID, candidate ID, rule/category mapping, and domain-qualified evidence. Every `CandidateV1.evidence` item is expanded to `(lesson.pattern.domainRef, runRef, seq)`; it must resolve to an observation in a complete, lossless supporting run from that same domain. The lesson pattern's run set and evidence coverage are revalidated rather than trusted.

The first evaluator accepts only `kind: 'prompt'`, maps it to `surface: 'template'`, requires `status: 'proposed'`, `evaluationRequired: true`, and `autoPromote: false`, and requires the candidate's `lessonId`, `hypothesis`, evidence, `baseConfigHash`, and `basePromptHash` to equal the resolved lesson/pattern values. The proposal's base artifact is a content-addressed `TemplateArtifactV1`; its binding digest covers the artifact reference, `configHash`, and `promptHash`. The candidate base hashes must exactly equal this governed base artifact's hashes, and the complete base artifact must exactly equal the currently active artifact. A modified candidate, pattern, or lesson ID, unresolved support, cross-domain support, or hash-to-artifact mismatch fails before ledger entry.

### 6. Seeded, hash-chained ledger

The ledger is initialized with an explicit active artifact for every governed surface. Every later entry contains a sequence number, entry ID, predecessor digest, transition kind, immutable decision metadata, and a complete proposal snapshot. The head digest is recomputed from canonical entry bytes. Active and last-known-good pointers are derived from the initial state plus the validated history; serialized pointers are not trusted as independent inputs. `lastKnownGood[surface]` means the rollback predecessor of the currently active promoted artifact, not an alias for the active artifact. For an initial artifact with no promotion predecessor, both pointers equal that initial artifact.

The allowed transitions are:

```text
proposed -> approved -> promoted -> rolled-back
         \\-> rejected
approved  -> rejected
```

`rejected` and `rolled-back` are terminal for that proposal. Rejection is allowed only from `proposed` or `approved`; it is never allowed after promotion. Promotion requires that the proposal base exactly equal the currently derived active artifact for its surface, preventing stale approved proposals from replacing a newer active artifact. Promotion records the prior active artifact as the proposal's immutable rollback target, sets `active` to the variant, and sets `lastKnownGood` to that target.

Rollback is allowed only for the promotion whose variant is currently active and only to that promotion's recorded target. It marks that proposal `rolled-back`, sets `active` to the target, and derives `lastKnownGood` from the target's own promotion predecessor; if the target is the seeded initial artifact, `lastKnownGood` equals the target. Thus, for seeded `A`, promoting `B` produces `(active=B, lastKnownGood=A)`; promoting `C` produces `(active=C, lastKnownGood=B)`; rolling back `C` to `B` produces `(active=B, lastKnownGood=A)`. A subsequent rollback of active `B` to `A` is valid using B's original promotion record. Re-promoting rolled-back `C` requires a new proposal and decision chain.

Every mutation request includes the expected next sequence and predecessor digest. An exact retry is idempotent only when its transition, proposal ID, expected sequence, expected predecessor, complete canonical proposal snapshot, and canonical decision record are byte-for-byte equal to an existing entry; `propose` uses its proposal ID in place of a null decision ID. The function then returns the input ledger unchanged and appends nothing. Decision IDs are globally unique. Reuse of a decision ID with any different actor, time, reason, transition, proposal, sequence, or predecessor is a conflicting duplicate and fails closed. The same transition attempted with a new decision ID is a new decision, not a retry; it must satisfy the current-state preconditions and therefore fails if the proposal has already left that source state. A sequence gap, predecessor mismatch, stale base, inconsistent derived pointer, non-active rollback, or wrong rollback target also fails closed without changing the ledger.

## Contracts

The implementation should add strict v1 contracts under `packages/dsh-eval/src/governance/`, export the pure subpath from `packages/dsh-eval/package.json`, and avoid re-exporting governance through the runtime-heavy main `packages/dsh-eval/src/index.ts` entry.

```ts
type GovernanceSurfaceV1 = 'template' | 'routing' | 'verification' | 'kernel'

type ArtifactRefV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  surface: GovernanceSurfaceV1
  version: string
  digest: string // 64 lowercase hexadecimal SHA-256 digest
}

type TemplateArtifactV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  ref: ArtifactRefV1 & { surface: 'template' }
  configHash: string
  promptHash: string
  bindingDigest: string // SHA-256 of canonical { schemaVersion, ref, configHash, promptHash }
}

type CorpusFixtureV1 = {
  fixtureId: string
  fixtureRevision: string
  pairingKey: string
}

type CorpusManifestV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  corpusId: 'template-offline-v1-corpus'
  corpusRevision: '1'
  fixtures: CorpusFixtureV1[]
  corpusManifestDigest: string
}

type EvaluationRunRefV1 = {
  domainRef: string
  runRef: string
  fixtureId: string
  fixtureRevision: string
  pairingKey: string
}

type EvaluationArmV1 = {
  artifact: TemplateArtifactV1
  fixtureRevision: string
  runs: EvaluationRunRefV1[]
  fixtureRuns: number
  resolvedRuns: number
  completeRuns: number
  incompleteRuns: number
  excludedRuns: number
  metrics: ArmMetricObservationV1[]
}

type ArmMetricObservationV1 = {
  name: string
  value: number | null
  numerator: number
  denominator: number
  observedRuns: number
  eligibleRuns: number
  basis: 'observed' | 'estimated' | 'unavailable'
}

type EvaluationPairV1 = {
  fixtureId: string
  fixtureRevision: string
  pairingKey: string
  baseRun: EvaluationRunRefV1
  variantRun: EvaluationRunRefV1
}

type ResolvedRunEvidenceV1 = {
  ref: EvaluationRunRefV1
  observations: ObservationV1[]
  seal: RunSealV1 | null
  annotation: RunAnnotationV1 | null
}

type OfflineEvidenceResolverInputV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  corpusManifest: CorpusManifestV1
  baseArtifact: TemplateArtifactV1
  variantArtifact: TemplateArtifactV1
  baseRuns: ResolvedRunEvidenceV1[]
  variantRuns: ResolvedRunEvidenceV1[]
}

type EvaluatorPolicyRefV1 = {
  policyId: 'template-offline-v1'
  policyRevision: 1
  policyDigest: string
}

type MetricComparisonV1 = {
  name: string
  baseValue: number | null
  variantValue: number | null
  delta: number | null
  result: 'pass' | 'fail' | 'unavailable'
}

type EvaluationEvidenceV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  policy: EvaluatorPolicyRefV1
  corpusId: string
  corpusRevision: string
  corpusManifestDigest: string
  resolverInput: OfflineEvidenceResolverInputV1
  baseArm: EvaluationArmV1
  variantArm: EvaluationArmV1
  pairs: EvaluationPairV1[]
  comparisons: MetricComparisonV1[]
  result: 'passed' | 'failed' | 'incomplete'
}

type DomainEvidenceRefV1 = {
  domainRef: string
  runRef: string
  seq: number
}

type CandidateSupportRunV1 = {
  domainRef: string
  runRef: string
  observations: ObservationV1[]
  seal: RunSealV1 | null
  annotation: RunAnnotationV1 | null
}

type CandidateSupportV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  lesson: LessonV1
  evidence: DomainEvidenceRefV1[]
  runs: CandidateSupportRunV1[]
}

type DecisionRecordV1 = {
  decisionId: string
  actorId: string
  occurredAtMs: number
  reasonDigest: string
}

type GovernanceProposalV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  proposalId: string
  candidate: CandidateV1
  candidateSupport: CandidateSupportV1
  bundleVersion: string
  policyGeneration: string
  surface: 'template'
  base: TemplateArtifactV1
  variant: TemplateArtifactV1
  evaluation: EvaluationEvidenceV1
  status: 'proposed' | 'approved' | 'rejected' | 'promoted' | 'rolled-back'
  approval: DecisionRecordV1 | null
  rejection: DecisionRecordV1 | null
  promotion: DecisionRecordV1 | null
  rollback: DecisionRecordV1 | null
  rollbackTarget: ArtifactRefV1 | null
}

type GovernanceEntryV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  sequence: number
  entryId: string
  predecessorDigest: string | null
  transition: 'propose' | 'approve' | 'reject' | 'promote' | 'rollback'
  decision: DecisionRecordV1 | null
  proposal: GovernanceProposalV1
}

type GovernanceLedgerV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1
  initialActive: Partial<Record<GovernanceSurfaceV1, TemplateArtifactV1>>
  entries: GovernanceEntryV1[]
  headDigest: string | null
}
```

All arrays above are bounded and canonically sorted. The concrete parser must use the package's established byte ceilings and reject unknown keys, malformed digests/references, unsupported candidate kinds, mismatched surface fields, duplicate manifest tuples, metric names, run keys, pair keys, support references, or decision IDs, negative or non-finite counts, invalid state/decision combinations, and non-canonical JSON. `deriveGovernanceState` must return the recomputed active and last-known-good maps and reject any inconsistent history.

The authoritative `template-offline-v1` policy and corpus manifest are code-owned checked-in constants. Serialized evidence embeds the resolver input for independent replay plus policy identity and derived assertions. `resolveOfflineEvidence` and `evaluateGovernanceEvidence` must recompute manifest identity, one-to-one fixture coverage, complete runs, counts, metrics, pair coverage, comparisons, and overall result before proposal or approval. `resolveCandidateSupport` must independently replay the candidate, lesson, pattern, support, and base-artifact binding checks.

## Data flow

1. Outside the governance dependency graph, existing candidate generation may emit an inert v0.5 hypothesis; governance receives only a supplied snapshot and never calls that builder.
2. Pure in-memory governance fixtures supply a seeded template artifact, prompt candidate plus lesson/pattern support snapshot, authoritative corpus manifest, and base/variant run evidence.
3. `resolveCandidateSupport` validates domain-qualified candidate support and binding to the governed base artifact.
4. `resolveOfflineEvidence` and `evaluateGovernanceEvidence` validate the manifest, both arms, complete run contents, exact pairing, policy identity, derived counts, metrics, comparisons, and result.
5. `proposeGovernance` validates the candidate snapshot, surface mapping, support resolution, artifact/evaluation agreement, and current active base.
6. `approveGovernance`, `rejectGovernance`, `promoteGovernance`, and `rollbackGovernance` append validated hash-chained entries and return new ledger values.
7. The dedicated governance subpath can serialize the ledger, but no current path writes a Profile, changes routing, changes permissions, accesses providers, or performs network I/O.

## Error handling

Invalid inputs fail closed with bounded, path-oriented validation errors that contain no raw prompt, source, provider, model, filesystem path, or private payload. State transitions reject rather than repair malformed history. Missing or unavailable evidence remains incomplete; it is never converted to a passing metric. Rollback failure leaves the input ledger unchanged.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Strict round-trip parsing and canonical serialization reject malformed, oversized, unknown-key, and inconsistent governance records.
2. A passing paired offline fixture evaluation proves exact one-to-one coverage of the authoritative manifest, domain-qualified arm references, valid pairing keys, resolved complete/lossless seals, observations and annotations, and evaluator-derived counts, metrics, comparisons, and result.
3. Symmetric fixture omission and missing or lossy seals produce `incomplete`; altered manifest identity, aggregates, metric observations, comparisons, or result are rejected as non-replayable assertions.
4. A prompt candidate snapshot can move `proposed -> approved -> promoted` only when its candidate, lesson, pattern and binding IDs recompute, all domain-qualified support resolves in-domain, and its base hashes match the currently active governed base artifact.
5. Modified IDs, unresolved support, cross-domain support, and candidate/base hash mismatches fail before proposal.
6. A promoted proposal can move to `rolled-back` only while its variant is active and only to its derived recorded target; wrong-target, missing-target, conflicting duplicate, stale-state, and predecessor-mismatch transitions fail closed.
7. Failed, incomplete, unavailable-required, mismatched, self-declared-policy, and duplicate evidence cannot be approved.
8. Repeated serialization and exact byte-equivalent retries are deterministic and idempotent; conflicting duplicates and new decisions against an already-consumed source state are rejected.
9. `A -> B -> C -> rollback-to-B` derives the exact active/last-known-good sequence specified above, and cascading `B -> A` rollback remains valid.
10. The built `@ds-plugins/dsh-eval/governance` subpath has no runtime/provider/filesystem imports, no dependency on the main eval entry, and no reuse of filesystem-writing candidate or lesson modules.
11. Tests prove no governed operation writes profiles, routes, permissions, credentials, network state, or provider data.
12. Existing v0.5 and v0.4 gates remain green, and the provider smoke remains the exact disabled keyless check.

## Required fixture matrix

The checked-in governance fixture must include:

- a seeded active template artifact and a valid prompt candidate;
- a canonical authoritative corpus manifest and paired base/variant runs with matching fixture IDs/revisions and domain-qualified references;
- a passing evaluation under the fixed policy;
- a failed threshold evaluation;
- an unavailable required metric and an incomplete arm;
- symmetric omission of one prescribed fixture, a missing seal, a lossy seal, and altered derived count/metric/comparison/result assertions;
- a candidate, lesson, and pattern ID mismatch; unresolved candidate support; cross-domain candidate support; base config/prompt hash mismatch; surface mismatch; duplicate pair; and mismatched corpus revision or manifest digest;
- two approved proposals sharing a stale base;
- the exact `A -> B -> C -> rollback-to-B` active/last-known-good sequence and cascading `B -> A` rollback;
- wrong-target, non-active rollback, stale-state, predecessor-digest, conflicting duplicate-decision, new-decision-after-consumed-state, and exact idempotent retry transitions.

## Deferred follow-up

After this slice is accepted, separately design: artifact publication and signatures, independent online A/B exposure accounting, quota/cost facts, coordinated multi-surface generations, runtime activation, and physical worker isolation. Each requires its own evidence and approval boundary.
