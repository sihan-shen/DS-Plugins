# DSH v0.6 Offline Governance Design

## Status

Proposed first v0.6 slice, revised after Sol/high and Astra reviews. This design starts controlled Self-Evolution as an offline governance capability; it does not activate runtime policy mutation.

## Goal

Turn the inert v0.5 candidate hypotheses into versioned, independently evaluated, human-approved promotion and rollback records while preserving the existing keyless and provider-disabled boundary.

## Scope

v0.6 first implements a pure governance layer in `@ds-plugins/dsh-eval`:

- a bounded policy-artifact reference and version schema;
- an authoritative evaluator policy for the first governed surface;
- explicit paired base/variant fixture evidence with domain-qualified run references;
- a full candidate snapshot whose identity and surface are independently verified;
- explicit `proposed`, `approved`, `rejected`, `promoted`, and `rolled-back` ledger states;
- immutable human decision records;
- deterministic hash-chained history with derived active and last-known-good pointers;
- offline tests over checked-in fixtures and replay evidence.

The first governed surface is `template`, mapped only from `CandidateV1.kind === 'prompt'`. The schema remains extensible to `routing`, `verification`, and `kernel`, but those surfaces are rejected by the first evaluator unless a separately versioned evidence policy is supplied.

## Non-goals

- No resident Self-Evolution plugin.
- No provider calls, credentials, network access, quota endpoint, or cost endpoint.
- No automatic runtime Profile, prompt, route, permission, sandbox, or verification-policy mutation.
- No online A/B assignment or exposure measurement; the first A/B comparison is paired offline replay over identical fixture inputs.
- No physical worker isolation, trusted write capture, or transactional workspace integration.
- No raw prompts, source text, model output, secrets, or private telemetry payloads in governance records.
- No reuse of the existing v0.2 `PromotionReportV1` as the complete v0.6 governance contract. It remains evaluation evidence for its original fixed corpus.

## Design choices

### 1. Pure governance subpath

The governance layer belongs beside the existing offline metrics and candidate generation, but it is exposed through a dedicated `@ds-plugins/dsh-eval/governance` subpath. The subpath imports only pure governance modules, canonical serialization, the validated telemetry contracts it needs, and deterministic SHA-256 primitives (for example `node:crypto`'s `createHash`). It must not import the main `dsh-eval` entry, runtime evaluators, Cordis, code-intelligence runtime code, filesystem readers or writers, provider adapters, or modules that also export filesystem-writing helpers. The compiled manifest and policy must be inert in-memory constants. These restrictions cover the entire static and dynamic import/re-export graph, including fixture helpers; replaying v0.5 behavior does not authorize imports from its miner, dataset, lesson, candidate, or metrics implementations.

In particular, governance must not import or call `telemetry/candidates.ts`, `telemetry/lessons.ts`, `buildCandidates`, `buildLessons`, `mineFailures`, `writeLesson`, or any fixture builder that reaches those modules. The governance package implements its own pure candidate-support resolver over supplied snapshots and telemetry records. Test fixture construction is pure and in-memory. The ledger consumes validated `CandidateV1` values and immutable evaluation evidence, returns new values, and has no Cordis lifecycle. File publication, signatures, and an artifact store are later concerns and must consume the same validated contracts rather than bypassing them.

```text
dsh-telemetry/contracts  ->  dsh-eval/governance
                                      |
                                      +-- pure contracts, evaluator policy, ledger
                                      +-- no runtime writer or provider import
```

### 2. Artifact references, not executable policy bodies

The first slice records opaque content-addressed artifact references. A reference contains a governed surface, semantic version, and SHA-256 digest of the canonical artifact supplied by an external authoring step. The ledger never interprets or executes the body.

### 3. Evaluator-owned policy

The first policy is the checked-in `template-offline-v1` evaluator policy. Its metric inventory, requiredness, availability rules, comparison formulas, directions, and thresholds are compiled constants identified by `(policyId, policyRevision, policyDigest)`. An input record carries only that policy identity; it cannot supply `required`, `threshold`, `passes`, or the overall result.

The evaluator derives all comparison statuses from the policy and arm observations. The parser rejects unknown policy identities and rejects any serialized derived field that does not equal recomputation under the known policy. An unavailable required metric contributes an `incomplete` condition and can never produce `passed`; an available metric failure still takes precedence and produces `failed`.

The normative `template-offline-v1` metric policy is:

| Metric | Required | Permitted arm basis | Minimum coverage (`observedRuns / eligibleRuns`) | Arm value formula | Direction and comparison | Pass threshold | Zero-denominator behavior |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| `task_success_rate` | yes | `observed` | `1.0` | successful known-outcome annotations / known-outcome annotations | higher is better; `delta = variantValue - baseValue` | `delta >= 0` | arm is `unavailable`; because required, overall is at best `incomplete` |
| `accepted_result_rate` | yes | `observed` | `1.0` | annotations with `accepted === true` / annotations with explicit boolean `accepted` | higher is better; `delta = variantValue - baseValue` | `delta >= 0` | arm is `unavailable`; because required, overall is at best `incomplete` |
| `verification_cost` | no | `observed` | `0.8` | sum of explicit `durationMs` on `verification-finished` observations / runs having at least one such explicit duration | lower is better; `delta = variantValue - baseValue` | `variantValue <= baseValue * 1.10` | comparison is `unavailable`; optional unavailability does not by itself prevent `passed` |

For every row, `eligibleRuns` is the number of complete prescribed fixture runs in that arm, except for the explicit overflow sentinel below. The numerator, denominator, `observedRuns`, `eligibleRuns`, basis, and value are recomputed from resolved observations and annotations; supplied values are assertions only. `value` must equal `numerator / denominator` without rounding when the denominator is positive. If the denominator is zero, the only canonical observation is `{ value: null, numerator: 0, denominator: 0, observedRuns: 0, eligibleRuns, basis: 'unavailable' }`, with the metric `name` included. A nonzero denominator with a disallowed basis or coverage below the row minimum makes that arm unavailable for comparison. The evaluator requires exactly these three metric names in this order and rejects extras, duplicates, and omissions.

Verification cost includes only `verification-finished` observations with an explicit `facts.durationMs`; absence is not zero, while an explicit zero counts as observed. Each duration must be a nonnegative safe integer under the v0.5 telemetry contract. Let `M = Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER = 9007199254740991`. Traverse complete runs in canonical arm order and observations by ascending `seq`, starting with `sum = 0`. Before every addition of `duration`, test `sum > M - duration`. If true, latch overflow for that entire arm; never publish a partial, rounded, saturated, wrapped, or later-recovered sum. Exactly `M` is valid. On overflow the metric must be exactly `{ name: 'verification_cost', value: null, numerator: 0, denominator: 0, observedRuns: 0, eligibleRuns: 0, basis: 'unavailable' }`, matching the v0.5 overflow sentinel. Arm completeness counts remain independently derived and are not zeroed. Either arm overflowing makes the comparison exactly `{ name: 'verification_cost', baseValue: null, variantValue: null, delta: null, result: 'unavailable' }`; the same all-null comparison shape applies whenever either arm is unavailable for any metric. Optional cost unavailability cannot hide a failure or required-metric unavailability under the result precedence. All available values, deltas, coverage ratios, and threshold products use finite ECMAScript binary64 arithmetic without rounding or tolerance adjustments.

Overall result precedence is exact: structural or derived-field inconsistency rejects the evidence; otherwise any available metric failure yields `failed`; otherwise incomplete corpus coverage, an incomplete/lossy run, or an unavailable required metric yields `incomplete`; otherwise the result is `passed`. An unavailable optional metric is ignored only at the last step.

`policyDigest` is the lowercase SHA-256 of canonical JSON for the following digest input, with `corpusManifestDigest` first recomputed as specified below. Property ordering is supplied by canonical serialization, not by source layout:

```ts
{
  schemaVersion: 1,
  policyId: 'template-offline-v1',
  policyRevision: 1,
  corpus: {
    corpusId: 'template-offline-v1-corpus',
    corpusRevision: '1',
    corpusManifestDigest: sha256(canonicalJson(corpusManifestBody)),
  },
  metrics: [
    { name: 'task_success_rate', required: true, permittedBasis: ['observed'], minimumCoverage: 1,
      formula: 'successful-known-outcomes/known-outcomes', direction: 'higher', threshold: { deltaGte: 0 },
      zeroDenominator: 'required-unavailable' },
    { name: 'accepted_result_rate', required: true, permittedBasis: ['observed'], minimumCoverage: 1,
      formula: 'accepted-true/explicit-acceptance', direction: 'higher', threshold: { deltaGte: 0 },
      zeroDenominator: 'required-unavailable' },
    { name: 'verification_cost', required: false, permittedBasis: ['observed'], minimumCoverage: 0.8,
      formula: 'verification-duration-ms/runs-with-verification-duration', direction: 'lower',
      threshold: { variantLteBaseTimes: 1.1 }, zeroDenominator: 'optional-unavailable',
      accumulation: { maximum: 9007199254740991, guard: 'sum>maximum-duration',
        overflow: 'latched-arm-unavailable-all-counts-zero', missingDuration: 'not-observed' } },
  ],
  requiredPairCoverage: 1,
  evidenceBinding: {
    armArtifactAnnotationHashes: 'base-exact-base-and-variant-exact-variant',
    fixtureTaskIdentity: 'exact-manifest-task-family-task-instance-and-input-digest',
    domainPairing: 'exact-manifest-ordered-base-variant-domain-pair',
    pairInclusion: 'both-prescribed-resolved-and-complete',
  },
  runAccounting: {
    fixtureRuns: 'manifest-fixture-count',
    resolvedRuns: 'prescribed-present-snapshot-count',
    completeRuns: 'prescribed-present-complete-snapshot-count',
    incompleteRuns: 'fixtureRuns-completeRuns',
    excludedRuns: 'zero',
  },
  arithmetic: 'ecmascript-binary64-no-rounding-no-tolerance',
  unavailableComparison: 'both-values-and-delta-null',
  overallResultPrecedence: ['reject-invalid', 'failed', 'incomplete', 'passed'],
}
```

### 4. Explicit paired arms

The checked-in `template-offline-v1-corpus` manifest is authoritative. Its exact hash body is `{ schemaVersion: 1, corpusId: 'template-offline-v1-corpus', corpusRevision: '1', fixtures }`, where `fixtures` is a non-empty, canonically sorted list of unique fixture records. A fixture record contains `(fixtureId, fixtureRevision, pairingKey, baseDomainRef, variantDomainRef, taskFamilyRef, taskInstanceRef, fixtureInputDigest)`. Its identity and ordering key is `(fixtureId, fixtureRevision, pairingKey)`; all remaining fields are immutable evidence-binding fields covered by the manifest digest. For compiled fixture definition `d`, let `fixtureInputBody = { schemaVersion: 1, fixtureId: d.fixtureId, fixtureRevision: d.fixtureRevision, taskFamilyRef: d.taskFamilyRef, taskInstanceRef: d.taskInstanceRef, input: d.input }`; the exact equation is `fixtureInputDigest = lowercaseHex(SHA256(UTF8(canonicalJson(fixtureInputBody))))`, with no newline. `d.input` is the complete plain-JSON input value supplied unchanged to both offline harness runs. The fixture author must assign `taskInstanceRef` to that exact input instance and pin both values; changing the input value or any identity/domain field requires a new fixture revision and corpus revision. Raw fixture input is code-owned test data and is not serialized into governance records. `corpusManifestDigest` is SHA-256 over UTF-8 canonical JSON of the manifest body, without a newline. Before implementing the revision-1 evaluator or publishing its digest, the implementation must check in the concrete manifest body and corresponding in-memory fixture definitions together, fixing the record inventory and fixture inputs for this policy revision. The fixture loader must recompute every `fixtureInputDigest` from those compiled definitions and require exact equality with the compiled manifest before resolving evidence. This design specifies that freeze point; it does not assign a digest to an uninstantiated corpus. The implementation acceptance gate must pin the exact canonical fixture-digest preimage bytes, manifest bytes, manifest digest, and resulting policy digest in golden fixtures.

The resolver must compare the supplied body byte-for-byte with that compiled checked-in body, recompute both digests, and compare them with the compiled policy identity. Matching a caller's self-consistent manifest/digest pair is insufficient. It must never construct the authoritative inventory or policy digest from caller-selected runs. Changing a fixture definition, tuple, ordering rule, metric formula, availability/overflow behavior, or threshold after the freeze requires a new policy revision and digest; corpus changes additionally require a new corpus revision and manifest digest. Revision 1 continues to mean only its original constants. Duplicate or non-canonical tuples are invalid, not a revision mechanism.

`EvaluationArmV1.fixtureRevision` is removed. Each arm instead carries `corpusRevision: '1'`, which must equal the authoritative manifest and enclosing evidence revision. Per-fixture `fixtureRevision` exists only on manifest tuples, run references, and pairs, and must match its tuple exactly; different fixtures may have different revisions in one corpus. The obsolete arm-level field is an unknown key and must be rejected.

`resolveOfflineEvidence` is a pure function from `OfflineEvidenceResolverInputV1` to `EvaluationEvidenceV1`. Its input contains the authoritative manifest and in-memory base and variant run snapshots. It performs no file reads, writes, environment access, clock reads, network calls, or provider calls. `baseArm.artifact` must canonically equal `resolverInput.baseArtifact`, and `variantArm.artifact` must canonically equal `resolverInput.variantArtifact`; when embedded in a proposal, those values must in turn canonically equal `proposal.base` and `proposal.variant`. For every present base snapshot that has an annotation, that annotation must have `configHash === baseArtifact.configHash` and `promptHash === baseArtifact.promptHash`; every present variant annotation must instead match the variant artifact's two hashes. A wrong-arm hash, swapped artifact assignment, or arm artifact assertion differing from its resolver/proposal artifact rejects the evidence rather than making the run incomplete.

For a manifest fixture `f`, a snapshot in arm `a` is prescribed only when its reference has exactly `f.fixtureId`, `f.fixtureRevision`, and `f.pairingKey`, and its `domainRef` equals `f.baseDomainRef` for the base arm or `f.variantDomainRef` for the variant arm. When present, its annotation must exactly match the snapshot's `(domainRef, runRef)`, `f.taskFamilyRef`, and `f.taskInstanceRef`, in addition to the arm artifact hashes above; an absent annotation instead makes the snapshot incomplete. `pairingKey` alone never establishes input identity. The compiled fixture definition whose digest is `f.fixtureInputDigest`, the manifest `taskInstanceRef`, and both annotations therefore identify one input; a different task family, task instance, or fixture input sharing a pairing key rejects. Cross-domain pairing is permitted only as the ordered pair `(baseRun.domainRef, variantRun.domainRef) === (f.baseDomainRef, f.variantDomainRef)`. The two domain refs may be equal; if they differ, reversing them or substituting either domain rejects. The base and variant `runRef` values may differ and must be domain-qualified and unique as described under canonical admission.

Passing evidence requires each arm to contain exactly one complete run for every manifest fixture and exactly one derived base/variant pair for every fixture. Unprescribed snapshots, duplicate snapshots or bindings, and identity mismatches reject. A prescribed snapshot may be omitted or may be present but incomplete; those cases are admitted as incomplete evidence so their counts and result can be derived. A fixture omitted symmetrically from both arms is still absent relative to the manifest and contributes an `incomplete` condition unless an available metric failure takes precedence; it never creates a smaller passing corpus.

A run is complete only when its domain-qualified reference matches every observation, its one seal, and its one annotation; the seal is present, has `complete === true`, `lostCount === 0`, and `observationCount === observations.length`; observation ordinals are unique and contiguous from 1 through `observationCount`; and every annotation evidence reference resolves to an observation in the same `(domainRef, runRef)`. An absent annotation, absent seal, lossy or incomplete seal, count mismatch, or ordinal gap makes that present snapshot incomplete. A present malformed record, conflicting duplicate, cross-run/domain record, unresolved annotation evidence, or annotation/fixture/artifact identity mismatch rejects, consistent with the v0.5 validators; it is not silently counted as an incomplete run.

Run accounting and pair derivation are exact. Let `F` be the authoritative ordered fixture list and `N = |F|`. For arm `a`, let `P_a(f)` mean that exactly one admitted snapshot prescribed for `f` is present, and let `C_a(f)` mean `P_a(f)` and that snapshot satisfies the complete-run predicate above. Uniqueness and admission rules make each predicate Boolean. The evaluator derives:

```text
fixtureRuns_a    = N
resolvedRuns_a   = sum(f in F, 1 when P_a(f), else 0)
completeRuns_a   = sum(f in F, 1 when C_a(f), else 0)
incompleteRuns_a = fixtureRuns_a - completeRuns_a
excludedRuns_a   = 0
missingRuns_a    = fixtureRuns_a - resolvedRuns_a // explanatory only; not serialized
```

Thus `0 <= completeRuns_a <= resolvedRuns_a <= fixtureRuns_a`, and `incompleteRuns_a` includes both omitted fixtures and present-but-incomplete snapshots. The derived `EvaluationArmV1.runs` array is exactly the references for which `P_a(f)` is true, in manifest order, so `runs.length === resolvedRuns_a`. An empty arm has `(fixtureRuns=N, resolvedRuns=0, completeRuns=0, incompleteRuns=N, excludedRuns=0)`. Symmetric omission decrements both resolved/complete counts; asymmetric omission decrements only the affected arm. A present-but-incomplete snapshot increments `resolvedRuns` but not `completeRuns`. Metrics use `eligibleRuns_a = completeRuns_a` except for the specified verification-overflow sentinel.

For each `f` in manifest order, include exactly one `EvaluationPairV1` iff `C_base(f) && C_variant(f)`; its tuple fields equal `f`'s identity key and its two run references are the corresponding prescribed snapshots. No complete snapshot may pair under another fixture's key, and no incomplete or omitted snapshot appears in `pairs`. Consequently `pairs.length = sum(f in F, 1 when C_base(f) && C_variant(f), else 0)`, and pair coverage is `pairs.length / N`. `requiredPairCoverage: 1` permits `passed` only when `pairs.length === N`; any smaller valid pair set contributes an `incomplete` condition. The resolver derives all arm counts, pairs, metrics, comparisons, and the overall result; serialized copies are assertions and reject if altered.

The initial paired offline A/B record is not an online exposure claim. It means both artifacts were evaluated against the same prescribed inputs under the same keyless harness.

### 5. Candidate snapshot and surface mapping

The proposal embeds the complete canonical `CandidateV1` snapshot, not only its ID, plus a `CandidateSupportV1` snapshot containing the complete lesson/pattern and the resolved supporting runs. The pure candidate-support resolver recomputes pattern ID, lesson ID, candidate ID, rule/category mapping, and domain-qualified evidence. Every `CandidateV1.evidence` item is expanded to `(lesson.pattern.domainRef, runRef, seq)`; it must resolve to an observation in a complete, lossless supporting run from that same domain. The lesson pattern's run set and evidence coverage are revalidated rather than trusted.

The first evaluator accepts only `kind: 'prompt'`, maps it to `surface: 'template'`, requires `status: 'proposed'`, `evaluationRequired: true`, and `autoPromote: false`, and requires the candidate's `lessonId`, `hypothesis`, evidence, `baseConfigHash`, and `basePromptHash` to equal the resolved lesson/pattern values. The proposal's base artifact is a content-addressed `TemplateArtifactV1`; its binding digest covers the artifact reference, `configHash`, and `promptHash`. The candidate base hashes must exactly equal this governed base artifact's hashes, and the complete base artifact must exactly equal the currently active artifact. A modified candidate, pattern, or lesson ID, unresolved support, cross-domain support, or hash-to-artifact mismatch fails before ledger entry.

#### Normative v0.5 support replay

The behavioral references are `packages/dsh-eval/src/telemetry/{dataset,miner,lessons,candidates}.ts` and their `tests/telemetry/*.spec.ts` tests. These are read-time references, never governance imports. `CandidateSupportV1.runs` declares the complete supplied eligible cohort pool against which selection is replayed, including eligible runs beyond the selected 32. It is not merely the selected run list. Governance proves eligibility and deterministic selection within this bounded supplied pool; it cannot attest that an external dataset contains no additional runs. The pool is part of the immutable proposal snapshot.

1. Parse each observation, seal, and annotation using the pure v0.5 contract validators. Every supplied pool run must be complete and lossless by section 4, have a non-null annotation with `outcome === 'failure'` and non-null `failure`, and resolve **both** `annotation.evidence` and `annotation.failure.evidence` to observations of that same `(domainRef, runRef)`. A success/unknown outcome, malformed annotation, duplicate run, or incomplete/unresolved pool member rejects candidate support; it must not be silently dropped to repair a claimed pool.
2. For `failure.attribution === 'reviewed'`, eligible evidence is the sorted, unique `failure.evidence` for any v0.5 `FAILURE_CLASSES` category. For `attribution === 'observed'`, the category must be `budget_exhaustion`; retain only references whose resolved observation has `kind === 'budget-rejected'`, and require at least one. Merely having a budget rejection elsewhere in the run is insufficient. No other observed category is eligible, and no causal attribution may be inferred from event kinds, statuses, or general annotation evidence. Other resolvable references may remain in the annotation, but cannot enter this observed support evidence set.
3. All pool annotations must share exactly `(domainRef, taskFamilyRef, configHash, promptHash, failure.category)` and those fields must equal the claimed pattern cohort. Require at least 3 distinct run references and 2 distinct `taskInstanceRef` values in that cohort before selection. A duplicate run cannot increase either count.
4. Sort eligible runs by ascending `runRef` and take the first `min(32, pool.length)`. If those runs contain only one task instance, replace the last selected run with the first later run having a different task instance, then sort the selection again. Reject if the selected set has fewer than 3 runs or 2 task instances. This is the v0.5 diversity-preserving selection, not an arbitrary subset.
5. Sort each selected run's eligible evidence by `(runRef, seq)` and seed the selected evidence set with the first reference from every selected run. Then traverse the sorted union of all selected runs' eligible references, adding references not already selected until the set reaches 32 or the union is exhausted. Sort the result by `(runRef, seq)`. Require 3..32 distinct evidence references and coverage of every selected run. The pattern's `runRefs` must equal the selected run list; `taskInstanceRefs` must equal its sorted unique task instances; pattern and candidate evidence must both equal the selected evidence list. `CandidateSupportV1.evidence` must equal that same list expanded with the cohort's `domainRef`, with no extra or missing reference.
6. Rebuild the pattern body with exactly `{ schemaVersion: 1, category, domainRef, taskFamilyRef, configHash, promptHash, runRefs, taskInstanceRefs, evidence }` and require `pattern.id === sha256(UTF8(canonicalJson(body)))`. The rule is `review-context-boundary` for `context_loss`, `insufficient_context`, or `context_bloat`; `review-verification-scope` for `verification_gap`; `review-route-fit` for `wrong_model`, `bad_routing`, or `prompt_incompatibility`; otherwise `review-failure-evidence`. Rebuild the lesson body as `{ schemaVersion: 1, revision: 1, pattern, rule }` and hash it without `id`. Rebuild the candidate body as `{ schemaVersion: 1, revision: 1, kind: 'prompt', status: 'proposed', lessonId: lesson.id, baseConfigHash: pattern.configHash, basePromptHash: pattern.promptHash, hypothesis: rule, evidence: pattern.evidence, evaluationRequired: true, autoPromote: false }` and hash it without `id`. Require exact canonical equality of each entire rebuilt snapshot with the supplied snapshot, not only matching IDs.

For these fixed-length lowercase hexadecimal references, ascending order is ASCII lexicographic order, agreeing with the v0.5 selections without dependence on host locale. The miner's set operations for per-run failure evidence remain applicable: embedded v0.5 annotation evidence arrays retain their original order and possible repeated references. Governance does not rewrite those annotations; only the derived pattern/candidate/support evidence lists must be sorted and unique.

### 6. Seeded, hash-chained ledger

The ledger is initialized with an explicit active artifact for every governed surface; this first evaluator requires exactly `{ template: TemplateArtifactV1 }` in `initialActive` and rejects other surface keys. Every later entry contains a sequence number, entry ID, predecessor digest, transition kind, immutable decision metadata, and a complete proposal snapshot. Active and last-known-good pointers are derived from the initial state plus the validated history; serialized pointers are not trusted as independent inputs. Derivation tracks an active **occurrence**, not merely an artifact reference, so an artifact that recurs in history is unambiguous. `lastKnownGood[surface]` means the artifact of the predecessor occurrence of the current active promotion occurrence, not an alias for an artifact's earliest or latest appearance. For the seeded occurrence, which has no promotion predecessor, both pointers equal the initial artifact.

#### Canonical chain identity

Let `H(x) = lowercaseHex(SHA256(UTF8(canonicalJson(x))))`, with no BOM, trailing newline, framing prefix, or implicit fields. The exact formulas are:

```ts
seedDigest = H({ schemaVersion: 1, initialActive: ledger.initialActive })
entryBody = { // exactly these keys; no entryId or headDigest
  schemaVersion: entry.schemaVersion,
  seedDigest,
  sequence: entry.sequence,
  predecessorDigest: entry.predecessorDigest,
  transition: entry.transition,
  decision: entry.decision,
  proposal: entry.proposal,
}
entry.entryId = H(entryBody)
ledger.headDigest = ledger.entries.length === 0 ? null : ledger.entries.at(-1)!.entryId
```

`seedDigest` is a derived preimage field, not a serialized entry property. The first entry has `sequence === 1` and `predecessorDigest === null`; entry at zero-based index `i > 0` has `sequence === i + 1` and `predecessorDigest === entries[i - 1].entryId`. `headDigest` equals the last `entryId` exactly; it is not another hash of the entry including its own ID. Recompute every ID from the validated seed and full post-transition snapshot, compare every predecessor, and replay every state transition before accepting the head. The seed is immutable for the lifetime of the ledger and covered by every entry hash. Hash chaining detects inconsistency relative to these supplied bytes; authenticating a replaced whole ledger or truncated history requires the deferred publication/signature boundary.

Entry identity validation requires the ledger's validated `initialActive` and preceding history as context; shape-only parsing of a standalone entry cannot certify its hash or transition. An input may not substitute a caller-asserted `seedDigest` for that context.

#### Immutable proposal and transition deltas

The immutable proposal fields are exactly `schemaVersion`, `proposalId`, `candidate`, `candidateSupport`, `bundleVersion`, `policyGeneration`, `surface`, `base`, `variant`, and `evaluation`, including every nested byte of their canonical snapshots. Every subsequent entry for a proposal must equal its first entry on those fields, even if replacement evidence independently passes or IDs have been recomputed. Changing any of them requires a fresh globally unique proposal ID and a new proposal/decision chain. The embedded v0.5 candidate always retains `status: 'proposed'`.

The only permitted changes relative to the latest snapshot for that proposal are:

| Transition | Source -> destination status | Fields initialized exactly once |
|---|---|---|
| `propose` | absent -> `proposed` | all four decision slots and `rollbackTarget` must be `null`; entry `decision` must be `null` |
| `approve` | `proposed` -> `approved` | `approval = entry.decision`, non-null; evaluation must replay to `passed` |
| `reject` | `proposed` or `approved` -> `rejected` | `rejection = entry.decision`, non-null; any existing approval is retained |
| `promote` | `approved` -> `promoted` | `promotion = entry.decision`, non-null; `rollbackTarget = priorActive.ref`, equal to immutable `base.ref`; passing evidence and approval remain required |
| `rollback` | `promoted` -> `rolled-back` | `rollback = entry.decision`, non-null; promotion, approval, and rollback target are retained |

All fields not listed as changing must remain canonically equal, including prior decision records and null slots. A decision slot cannot be cleared, overwritten, moved, or backfilled. A non-null entry decision must canonically equal the slot initialized by that entry. Append operations leave previous entries and caller-owned inputs unchanged and return detached snapshots, preventing later caller mutation from altering history. Decision-ID uniqueness applies to decision-creating entries; copies of an earlier decision in later snapshots of the same proposal are required retention, not duplicate decisions. Validate full history before the retry check; an exact retry is the sole exception to new proposal/decision ID uniqueness and current source-state checks, and cannot bypass history validation.

The allowed transitions are:

```text
proposed -> approved -> promoted -> rolled-back
         \\-> rejected
approved  -> rejected
```

`rejected` and `rolled-back` are terminal for that proposal. Rejection is allowed only from `proposed` or `approved`; it is never allowed after promotion. Promotion requires that the proposal base exactly equal the currently derived active artifact for its surface, preventing stale approved proposals from replacing a newer active artifact. A proposal whose variant canonically equals the current active artifact is a no-op and rejects; reintroduction is permitted only after that artifact has ceased to be active. Promotion records the prior active artifact as the proposal's immutable rollback target, creates a new promotion occurrence for the variant, sets `active` to that occurrence's artifact, and sets `lastKnownGood` to its predecessor occurrence's artifact.

Occurrence replay is exact. For each surface, initialize `currentOccurrence = { artifact: initialActive[surface], promotionSequence: null, promotionProposalId: null, predecessor: null }`. Replaying a valid `promote` entry `e` replaces it with `{ artifact: e.proposal.variant, promotionSequence: e.sequence, promotionProposalId: e.proposal.proposalId, predecessor: priorCurrentOccurrence }`. The predecessor is an internal history-derived link; it is not caller-supplied and need not be serialized. The proposal's serialized `rollbackTarget` must canonically equal `priorCurrentOccurrence.artifact.ref`. The active artifact is `currentOccurrence.artifact`; `lastKnownGood` is `currentOccurrence.predecessor?.artifact ?? currentOccurrence.artifact`.

Rollback first locates the unique prior `promote` entry for that proposal and derives its occurrence. It is allowed only when both `currentOccurrence.promotionSequence` and `currentOccurrence.promotionProposalId` equal that promotion entry's sequence and proposal ID, and when the proposal's retained `rollbackTarget` equals `currentOccurrence.predecessor.artifact.ref`. Artifact equality alone is insufficient. A valid rollback marks that proposal `rolled-back` and replaces `currentOccurrence` with its exact predecessor occurrence, preserving that occurrence's own promotion identity and predecessor link; it then recomputes both public pointers from the restored occurrence. This prevents a rollback for an earlier occurrence of artifact `B` from acting on a later reintroduction of the same `B`.

Thus, for seeded `A`, promoting `B` produces `(active=B, lastKnownGood=A)`; promoting `C` produces `(active=C, lastKnownGood=B)`; rolling back `C` restores the original `B` occurrence and produces `(active=B, lastKnownGood=A)`. A subsequent rollback of that active `B` occurrence to `A` is valid using B's original promotion record. Recurrence is also explicit: `A(seed) -> B(occurrence 1) -> C -> B(occurrence 2)` makes occurrence 2 active with predecessor `C`; rolling back occurrence 2 restores `C`, and a rollback request for occurrence 1 rejects even though its variant artifact is also `B`. Likewise `A(seed) -> B -> A(reintroduced)` gives the reintroduced `A` a predecessor of `B`; it is not treated as the seed, and rolling it back restores `B`. Re-promoting a rolled-back artifact requires a new proposal and decision chain and creates a new occurrence.

Every mutation request includes the expected next sequence and predecessor digest. An exact retry is idempotent only when its transition, proposal ID, expected sequence, expected predecessor, complete canonical proposal snapshot, and canonical decision record are byte-for-byte equal to an existing entry; `propose` uses its proposal ID in place of a null decision ID. The function then returns the input ledger unchanged and appends nothing. Decision IDs are globally unique. Reuse of a decision ID with any different actor, time, reason, transition, proposal, sequence, or predecessor is a conflicting duplicate and fails closed. The same transition attempted with a new decision ID is a new decision, not a retry; it must satisfy the current-state preconditions and therefore fails if the proposal has already left that source state. A sequence gap, predecessor mismatch, stale base, inconsistent derived pointer, non-active rollback, or wrong rollback target also fails closed without changing the ledger.

### Canonical input admission

Canonical JSON means exactly the existing pure telemetry `canonicalJson` format: object keys recursively sorted by ECMAScript string order, array order preserved, primitive spelling from `JSON.stringify`, well-formed Unicode, and no whitespace outside strings. It is not an unspecified canonicalization standard. All SHA-256 inputs in this document use its UTF-8 bytes without a newline, including artifact binding, manifest, policy, pattern, lesson, candidate, seed, and entry hashes.

A raw JSON entry point must first enforce the raw byte and structural admission limits below, decode UTF-8 with fatal errors, reject a BOM, parse exactly one JSON value, validate its strict schema without coercion or repair, and require `inputBytes === UTF8(canonicalJson(validatedValue))` byte-for-byte. Do not trim input or remove a final newline. This equality rejects duplicate object keys (including equal duplicates and escape-equivalent keys), reordered keys, alternate numeric spellings (`1.0`, `1e0`, `-0`), alternate string escapes, and whitespace even when ordinary `JSON.parse` produces an equivalent value. Duplicate keys must not be accepted simply because a parser kept the last value. Raw trailing content, malformed UTF-8, and lone surrogates reject. No Unicode normalization is performed.

Object entry points cannot prove how an already parsed value was spelled. They must validate detached plain JSON data, exact keys, required fields, array ordering, bounds, and derived values, then serialize canonically; they must not claim to have checked prior raw bytes. Reject cyclic/non-plain values, sparse arrays, named array properties, symbols, accessors, non-enumerable data fields other than array `length`, `undefined`, functions, bigint, non-finite numbers, and negative zero before serialization. Do not invoke getters or `toJSON` hooks. Raw-input APIs must not route through a path that loses the original bytes before the equality check. Supplied arrays are checked, not sorted or truncated by the parser; derived arrays are constructed in their prescribed order.

Array ordering and uniqueness are exact:

- Manifest fixtures, arm run references, resolver arm snapshots (by `ref`), and pairs use ascending `(fixtureId, fixtureRevision, pairingKey)` tuple order. Compare each component lexicographically by ASCII code point. Tuple keys must be unique; `(domainRef, runRef)` must also be unique within each arm, and the same domain-qualified run must not be reused across arms or bound to multiple tuples.
- Candidate pool runs use `(domainRef, runRef)` order; observations use ascending numeric `seq`, unique within their run. Pattern `runRefs` and `taskInstanceRefs` are sorted unique reference lists. Derived evidence uses `(runRef, seq)` or `(domainRef, runRef, seq)` as appropriate. Embedded v0.5 annotation evidence arrays are the sole set-like exception: preserve their original order and duplicates, within the 32-reference bound, and replay the miner's deduplication as specified in section 5.
- Arm metrics and comparisons have exactly the policy's three entries in policy order, not alphabetic order. Ledger entries use contiguous sequence order. Policy arrays have exactly the order shown in the digest input, including singleton basis lists and the four result-precedence entries; they are compiled constants, not caller-controlled lists.

### Explicit v1 limits

All limits are inclusive compiled governance-v1 admission constants, not caller settings. A lower enclosing byte/count limit always wins; exceeding any limit rejects without truncation, dropping runs, or appending a partial transition. The established v0.5 record, dataset, annotation, and output ceilings are retained, with additional governance-specific bounds:

| Resource / accumulator | Maximum and admission rule |
|---|---|
| Any raw governance JSON document or canonical serialized top-level value, including a ledger, entry, proposal, evaluation, support snapshot, manifest, or transition request | 16,777,216 UTF-8 bytes (16 MiB); a request containing both a ledger and proposed data counts both |
| Any nested proposal, entry, evaluation, support snapshot, or resolver input | 16,777,216 canonical UTF-8 bytes; enclosing limits still apply and repeated snapshots count every occurrence |
| JSON traversal | Container nesting depth 32 (root container depth 1), 1,048,576 value nodes (including containers), 32 own fields per object, 128 UTF-8 bytes per key; reject unknown keys at every schema level |
| Strings | Every non-key string at most 128 UTF-8 bytes; hashes and all telemetry references exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters; other IDs/revisions/`actorId`/`bundleVersion`/`policyGeneration` match `^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._-]{0,127}$`; enums and literal versions must additionally match their types |
| Artifact `version` | At most 128 ASCII bytes, valid SemVer 2.0.0 including its no-leading-zero rules; major/minor/patch are nonnegative safe integers; this grammar replaces the generic identifier grammar for this field |
| Manifest fixtures, each arm's run references and resolver snapshots, pairs | Manifest 1..256 tuples; each other array 0..256 and no more than manifest length; omission remains `incomplete`, not an admission error |
| Candidate support pool | 3..4,096 distinct runs before selection; selected pattern runs 3..32; selected task instances 2..32; pattern/candidate/domain-qualified support evidence each 3..32 |
| Embedded annotation evidence | `annotation.evidence` and non-null `failure.evidence` each 1..32, as in the v0.5 contract |
| Observations | Each run 0..2,048; at most 65,536 telemetry records (observations plus non-null seals) across candidate support and both evaluation arms of one proposal |
| Telemetry bytes | Each observation/seal satisfies `UTF8(canonicalJson(record)).length + 1 <= 8,192`; the sum of those charged lengths across one proposal is at most 8,388,608 (8 MiB) |
| Annotations | At most 4,096 non-null annotations across one proposal; each canonical annotation plus one charged LF byte at most 8,192 bytes; the canonical array of all such annotations, including brackets and commas, at most 1,048,576 bytes (1 MiB) |
| Standalone support / resolver / evaluation inputs | Apply the same combined telemetry/annotation caps to all contained runs; when embedded, apply the caps again across the whole proposal, not separately per arm |
| Ledger entries and identity indexes | 0..1,024 entries; at most 1,024 distinct proposal IDs and 1,024 decision-creating entries; derived active/last-known-good maps have exactly one `template` key |
| Count, ordinal, duration, and timestamp inputs | Nonnegative safe integers at most `M`; ledger sequence is 1..1,024 and must equal its index plus one; durations and supplied timestamps may be 0..`M`; ordinals must be 1..2,048 for a complete run |
| Derived run counts / rate numerators and denominators | 0..256, bounded further by the authoritative manifest and complete/observed run counts; `observedRuns <= eligibleRuns`; verification denominator is the number of complete runs with explicit duration, at most 256 |
| Derived metric values and comparison arithmetic | Finite binary64 fractions are permitted: rates/coverage 0..1, cost 0..`M`, rate deltas -1..1, cost delta -`M`..`M`, cost threshold product 0..`M * 1.1`; exact recomputation and unavailable nulls remain mandatory |
| Duration accumulator | 0..`M`, checked before every addition; the overflow sentinel in section 3 is the only metric-specific exception to rejecting unsafe accumulation |
| Other counters, byte totals, indexes, sets, maps, and temporary selection arrays | Never exceed the corresponding admitted source count or byte cap above; a cross-run evidence union has at most `4,096 * 32 = 131,072` input references and a selected-run union at most `32 * 32 = 1,024`; final selected evidence at most 32; never allocate using a claimed count without bounding it |
| Error output | Stop at the first error; at most 512 UTF-8 bytes using fixed schema paths, bounded numeric indexes, and fixed reason codes, never caller-supplied keys or values |

Use checked addition for every count/byte accumulator: for nonnegative safe integers `total`, `increment`, and its limit `L <= M`, require `increment <= L` and `total <= L - increment` before computing the next total. Check multiplication before allocation using `a === 0 || b <= Math.floor(L / a)` for nonnegative safe integers. Bound lengths, depth, and node counts before copying, recursive validation, sorting, hashing, or building indexes; a bounded lexical preflight must enforce raw JSON nesting/nodes/collection sizes before full parsing. Incremental canonical byte accounting must stop at the applicable byte cap instead of first allocating unbounded serialization. A serializer must reject an oversized output even when all input fragments separately fit. Limit checks include duplicate/repeated occurrences before any set operation. Transitions must check prospective ledger length/bytes before append and return the unchanged input on failure; a validated exact retry appends nothing and remains valid at the capacity limit.

The parser must also reject unsupported candidate kinds, mismatched surfaces, invalid state/decision combinations, malformed references, inconsistent derived fields, and conflicting identities. `deriveGovernanceState` must return the recomputed active and last-known-good maps and reject any inconsistent history.

The authoritative `template-offline-v1` policy and corpus manifest are code-owned checked-in constants. Serialized evidence embeds the resolver input for independent replay plus policy identity and derived assertions. `resolveOfflineEvidence` and `evaluateGovernanceEvidence` must recompute manifest identity, one-to-one fixture coverage, complete runs, counts, metrics, pair coverage, comparisons, and overall result before proposal or approval. `resolveCandidateSupport` must independently replay the candidate, lesson, pattern, support, and base-artifact binding checks.

## Data flow

1. Outside the governance dependency graph, existing candidate generation may emit an inert v0.5 hypothesis; governance receives only a supplied snapshot and never calls that builder.
2. Pure in-memory governance fixtures supply a seeded template artifact, prompt candidate plus lesson/pattern support snapshot, authoritative corpus manifest, and base/variant run evidence.
3. `resolveCandidateSupport` validates domain-qualified candidate support and binding to the governed base artifact.
4. `resolveOfflineEvidence` and `evaluateGovernanceEvidence` validate the manifest, both arms, complete run contents, exact pairing, policy identity, derived counts, metrics, comparisons, and result.
5. `proposeGovernance` validates the candidate snapshot, surface mapping, support resolution, artifact/evaluation agreement, and current active base.
6. `approveGovernance`, `rejectGovernance`, `promoteGovernance`, and `rollbackGovernance` append validated hash-chained entries and return new ledger values.
7. The dedicated governance subpath can serialize the ledger, but no current path writes a Profile, changes routing, changes permissions, accesses providers, or performs network I/O.

## Error handling

Invalid inputs fail closed with bounded, path-oriented validation errors that contain no raw prompt, source, provider, model, filesystem path, or private payload. State transitions reject rather than repair malformed history. Missing, incomplete, or required-unavailable evidence contributes an `incomplete` condition and is never converted to passing; after structural validation, however, any available metric failure takes precedence and the combined result is `failed`. Rollback failure leaves the input ledger unchanged.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Strict round-trip parsing and canonical serialization reject malformed, oversized, unknown-key, and inconsistent governance records.
2. A passing paired offline fixture evaluation proves exact one-to-one coverage of the authoritative manifest, exact arm-to-artifact annotation hashes, fixture task/input identity, the manifest-authorized ordered domain pair, resolved complete/lossless seals, observations and annotations, and evaluator-derived counts, metrics, pairs, comparisons, and result.
3. Symmetric or asymmetric fixture omission, an empty arm, and present-but-incomplete snapshots derive the exact run equations and produce `incomplete` unless an available metric failure takes precedence; altered manifest identity, aggregates, metric observations, pairs, comparisons, or result are rejected as non-replayable assertions.
4. A prompt candidate snapshot can move `proposed -> approved -> promoted` only when its candidate, lesson, pattern and binding IDs recompute, all domain-qualified support resolves in-domain, and its base hashes match the currently active governed base artifact.
5. Modified IDs, unresolved support, cross-domain support, and candidate/base hash mismatches fail before proposal.
6. A promoted proposal can move to `rolled-back` only while its exact promotion occurrence is active and only to that occurrence's history-derived predecessor; artifact equality alone, a wrong/missing target, conflicting duplicate, stale state, and predecessor mismatch fail closed.
7. Failed, incomplete, unavailable-required, mismatched, self-declared-policy, and duplicate evidence cannot be approved.
8. Repeated serialization and exact byte-equivalent retries are deterministic and idempotent; conflicting duplicates and new decisions against an already-consumed source state are rejected.
9. `A -> B -> C -> rollback-to-B` derives the exact active/last-known-good sequence specified above, cascading `B -> A` rollback remains valid, and repeated or seeded-artifact reintroductions restore the predecessor occurrence rather than another occurrence with the same artifact.
10. The built `@ds-plugins/dsh-eval/governance` subpath has no runtime/provider/filesystem imports, no dependency on the main eval entry, and no reuse of filesystem-writing candidate or lesson modules.
11. Tests prove no governed operation writes profiles, routes, permissions, credentials, network state, or provider data.
12. Existing v0.5 and v0.4 gates remain green, and the provider smoke remains the exact disabled keyless check.
13. Golden chain fixtures pin exact seed/entry preimage bytes and SHA-256 digests, including the empty ledger and first entry; the head equals the last entry ID. History replay rejects tampering even when an attacker recomputes affected IDs but violates proposal immutability or transition deltas.
14. Candidate support reproduces the v0.5 failure-only, attribution, cohort, minimum-support, diversity-preserving bounded selection, evidence selection, rule mapping, and identity rules. Rehashed but ineligible snapshots fail before proposal; no v0.5 builder or filesystem dependency is introduced to accomplish replay.

Supplied bodies must equal the compiled body; fixture input digests, task identities, ordered domains, and arm artifact hashes are exact; mixed per-fixture revisions are supported, arm `corpusRevision` must match, and the removed arm `fixtureRevision` is rejected.
16. Verification overflow at `M + 1` produces the exact all-zero unavailable sentinel and all-null comparison; exact `M`, explicit zero, absent duration, optional/required availability, and overall precedence behave as specified. A combined available failure plus incomplete or required-unavailable condition is `failed`. Golden policy digest assertions include the evidence-binding, run-accounting, overflow, arithmetic, and unavailable-comparison constants.
17. Raw canonicality is checked against original bytes, while object APIs validate structure without claiming raw provenance. Every ordering rule and every row of the limits table has boundary and rejection fixtures, including cumulative limits, bounded errors, safe arithmetic, and capacity-limited append/retry behavior.
18. Import-graph and caller-mutation tests verify the pure subpath, in-memory constants, detached snapshots, and absence of forbidden static/dynamic imports or re-exports. Missing ledger context cannot certify a standalone entry's identity.
